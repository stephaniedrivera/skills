Writing style for every prose surface: docs, PRs, chat, customer and team messages, code comments, and other human-readable text. Code and identifiers are exempt.
 
 
 
When used on an existing piece of prose, rewrite it using this style.
 
## Rules
 
- Write in plain, natural English.
- Lead with the main point.
- Prefer short, active sentences. Keep one idea per sentence when it sounds natural.
- Cut repetition and words that do not change the meaning. Prefer direct, concrete wording when equally precise.
- Describe the result, not the editing process. Include history only when it affects current behavior or compatibility.
- Preserve technical terms, numbers, qualifications, and causal claims.
- Prefer a short everyday word when it is as precise. Do not add jargon. Do not replace a technical term with a simpler paraphrase.
- Do not add facts, causes, tests, or guarantees that the source does not support.
- Avoid clichés and em dashes.
- Prefer accuracy and clarity over brevity when they conflict.
- Opinions, empathy, humor, and first person are content, not decoration. Use them only when the request, source, or speaker supports them.
- Avoid puffery, promotional language, vague attribution, chatbot pleasantries, sycophancy, generic conclusions, forced contrasts, rule-of-three lists, synonym cycling, false ranges, superficial `-ing` phrases, and decorative emojis.
- Keep the source's genre and register. Making prose human does not mean making it casual.
- When editing existing prose, preserve language that is already specific and prefer the smallest effective edit.
